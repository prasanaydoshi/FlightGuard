What the model knows—and what it does not

Two logistic models use only scheduled layover, outbound departure hour at ATL, inbound departure weekday, and origin/destination. January fits the model, February 3–28 calibrates its scores, and March 3–31 evaluates it. The first two days of February and March are embargoed. No actual delays, cancellation outcomes, or future weather enter the predictors.

Connection failure means either flight was canceled/diverted, or actual inbound arrival plus 30 minutes exceeds actual outbound gate departure minus 15 minutes. Disruption additionally includes outbound arrival over 60 minutes late. These are modeling assumptions, not airline minimum-connection or boarding rules.

The sample covers DL-operated flights between ATL and BOS, DEN, LAX, ORD, SEA. For each inbound/destination, only the earliest and latest scheduled onward departures 45–240 minutes after arrival are considered. There are no passenger booking records, gate or terminal data, seats, fares, rebooking outcomes, baggage, or accessibility needs. March results do not establish performance in other months, carriers or airports. Data were retrieved September 6, 2026 and reflect revised BTS reports, not an archived booking-time timetable.
